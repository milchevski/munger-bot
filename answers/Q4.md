### Q: Should I invest in China?

That's a question Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger have touched upon several times, and their insights offer a nuanced perspective.

Here's a breakdown of their views:

**Warren Buffett's Perspective:**

*   **Universal Investment Principles:** Buffett firmly believes that sound investment principles do not stop at borders. Whether you're investing in China, India, the UK, or Germany, he would apply "exactly the same sort of principles" he learned from "The Intelligent Investor." This means thinking of stocks as small pieces of a business, using market fluctuations to your advantage, and focusing on businesses with understandable competitive advantages and long-term prospects (2015).
*   **Opportunities for Value Investors:** He suggests that if you can find "really good businesses" in China – those earning high returns on capital (like 20% on equity), selling at low multiples (e.g., five times earnings), with good quality earnings, and the ability to reemploy a significant portion of those earnings at high rates – you will "make a lot of money" (1999). He even goes as far as to say that if you can identify such companies, you "will do much better in that than you will in American markets" (1999).
*   **China's Economic Miracle:** Buffett is deeply impressed by China's economic transformation over the past 40-50 years, calling it "totally miraculous." He views it as a powerful example of unleashing human potential and believes China and the United States will be the superpowers for the foreseeable future, emphasizing the importance of mutual success (2015).
*   **Challenges for Berkshire's Size:** While seeing opportunities, Buffett notes that for Berkshire, accumulating "many billions" into something in an unfamiliar market like China can be tough due to its sheer size and reporting requirements (2018).
*   **PetroChina Example:** He points to Berkshire's successful investment in PetroChina, which was bought after simply reading its annual report (in English) at a very attractive valuation (about three times earnings) when it was significantly undervalued compared to other international oil companies (2005). This illustrates that opportunities can be found through diligent research, even without direct management contact.

**Charlie Munger's Perspective:**

*   **"American Investors Are Missing China":** Munger is even more direct, stating that "American investors are missing China." He attributes this to distance, cultural differences, complexity, and confusing headlines, making it seem "too hard" from Omaha. However, he believes it's "where they should be looking" (2018).
*   **High on China's Future:** Munger is a "big fan" of what's happening in China, praising its economic progress and its efforts to curb corruption, drawing parallels to Lee Kuan Yew's success in Singapore. He considers these actions "the smartest damn thing I've seen a big country do in a long, long time" and believes China's path is "very likely to work" (2015).
*   **Volatility Creates Opportunity:** He acknowledges China's history of being very entrepreneurial and prone to heavy gambling, which has created "great volatility" in its stock markets. While he wishes China would adopt more value investing principles and less speculation, he also notes that this volatility "should produce great opportunities" for those who can capitalize on "excess and nasty contractions after unnatural booms" (2015).
*   **Value Investing is Timeless:** Munger emphatically states that "value investing will never go out of style" and that it's the only sensible approach when buying something. He warns against looking for an "easier way," as it often leads to "misery" (2015).

**In Summary:**

Both Buffett and Munger are bullish on China's long-term economic prospects and see significant investment opportunities there. They emphasize that the core principles of value investing—understanding the business, buying at a discount to intrinsic value, and controlling your emotions—are paramount and apply just as much, if not more, in China. Munger, in particular, believes American investors are overlooking a fertile ground.

However, they also imply that identifying specific "winners" requires expertise and diligence, especially given the market's potential for greater speculation and volatility. For Berkshire, the challenge is finding opportunities large enough to move the needle. For individual investors, the message is clear: if you apply sound value investing principles and are willing to do the homework, China could offer compelling opportunities.
